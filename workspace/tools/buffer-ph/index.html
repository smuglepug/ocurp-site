Buffer pH

Henderson-Hasselbalch against the useful pKa +- 1 band, and the exact charge-balance pH for a real recipe.

Two ways to the same pH. Ratio mode applies Henderson-Hasselbalch to a pair you have already measured. Recipe mode takes what you actually weighed into the flask and solves the charge balance for the real pH as an exact quadratic, instead of assuming the two species sit in their analytical ratio. Both flag the ratio when it leaves the 0.1 to 10 window where a buffer actually buffers.

Mode
Ratio mode takes two concentrations you have already measured or titrated to, and reports the Henderson-Hasselbalch pH directly.
Recipe mode takes what you weighed out into the flask and solves the charge balance for the real pH instead of assuming the two species are already in their analytical ratio. See the derivation note below.
pH
Working
Useful-range window
A conjugate pair only buffers usefully while both members are present in comparable amounts. The usual working window is a ratio between 0.1 and 10, which is pKa -/+ 1. Outside that the buffer capacity on one side of the pH has collapsed, and the pH you read will drift hard with a drop of titrant even though the arithmetic still returns a number.
Read-out

  

    Where recipe mode's quadratic comes from.
    For Ca mol/L of HA plus Cs mol/L of NaA the total acetate is Ct = Ca + Cs.
    Charge balance gives [A-] = h + Cs - Kw/h and mass balance then gives
    [HA] = Ct - [A-] = Ca - h + Kw/h; notice the salt cancels out of [HA],
    because the sodium ions are what balance the protons the acid gives up.
    Substituting into Ka = h[A-]/[HA] and multiplying through by h collects
    into a cubic in h that carries the water term Kw. Setting only the Kw term
    to zero - the one approximation here - leaves the exact quadratic
    h^2 + (Ka + Cs) h - Ka Ca = 0. It is solved in the numerically stable
    rationalised form h = 2 Ka Ca / ((Ka + Cs) + sqrt((Ka +Cs)^2 + 4 Ka Ca)),
    never as (-b + sqrt(disc))/2, because that subtraction cancels to zero when
    Ca is small or comparable to Cs. Kw = 1e-14 at 25 C; the water term shifts
    the answer by less than 1e-4 pH over the whole useful range, which is why
    dropping it is safe and why it is dropped.
