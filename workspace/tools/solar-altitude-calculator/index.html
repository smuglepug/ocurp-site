Solar Altitude Calculator

ASTRONOMY — CLIENT-SIDE

Where the Sun is in the sky, from latitude, longitude, date and time. The equation of time and the Sun's declination come from the NOAA fractional-year approximations; the hour angle is the true solar time converted at 15° per hour; the altitude and azimuth follow from the standard spherical relations. Sunrise, sunset and civil twilight are solved for as events rather than assumed from a day-length figure, and above the Arctic circle in winter the page reports polar night instead of inventing a sunrise that does not happen. Sunrise uses a horizon of −0.833° — the Sun's own disc plus standard refraction — which is why the day at the equator is twelve hours and seven minutes rather than twelve exactly, and the table below traces the year's day length at this latitude.

Sunrise and sunset use a horizon altitude of −0.833° (the Sun's own radius plus standard refraction), not 0°, which is why day length at the equator is 12 h 7 min rather than exactly 12 h. Civil twilight is −6° and astronomical twilight −18°. Times are UTC plus your offset. This is the NOAA approximation and it is good to about a minute for sunrise; it ignores the observer's elevation.
