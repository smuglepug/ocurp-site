Doppler Shift Calculator

science — CLIENT-SIDE

Two genuinely different formulas that share a name. Acoustic Doppler handles a moving source and a moving observer in a real medium; the relativistic longitudinal shift handles light, where the whole thing collapses to sqrt((1-b)/(1+b)). The page runs one or the other and shows what the wrong one would have said, because the classic mistake is feeding the speed of light into the acoustic ratio.

Why the two formulas must not be mixed
Acoustic Doppler treats the medium as a physical reference frame: the source physically compresses the wavefronts it leaves behind, so the spacing changes by the source speed while the observer's motion only changes how fast the crests are met. Relativistic Doppler is the same observed effect for light, where there is no medium, and it collapses to a single factor sqrt((1-b)/(1+b)) with b = v/c on the relative speed, b < 1 strictly. Substituting the light speed into the acoustic ratio is not an approximation that gets better at high speed, it is a different quantity that grows without bound as the source passes the observer, which is why the wrong formula would hand back a superluminal frequency.
