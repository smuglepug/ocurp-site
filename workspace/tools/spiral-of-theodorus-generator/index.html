Spiral of Theodorus Generator

Successive right triangles on the current radius: the radius after n steps, the exact cumulative angle and growth against sqrt(n), drawn on canvas.

The square-root spiral, built from nothing but right triangles. Start with a line segment; draw a second at right angles to it, join their tips with a hypotenuse, then build the next triangle on that hypotenuse. After n steps the distance from the centre is not merely close to leg·√(n+1) — it is exactly that, because every step squares the radius and adds one leg squared, and no approximation is involved anywhere. The angle is the other story: each step sweeps atan(leg / √k), which has no closed form, so the total is accumulated term by term and it is that slow divergence that makes the arms overlap after about eight turns' worth of steps. The page draws the spiral, lists every radius and angle, and shows r/√n approaching the leg from above.

Each new triangle puts the previous radius in one leg and the shared leg in the other, so rn² = leg² + rn−1² and by induction rn = leg·√(n+1) exactly — not approximately. The angle swept at step k is atan(leg / √k), with no closed form, so the total is accumulated term by term.
