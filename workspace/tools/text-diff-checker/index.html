Text Diff Checker

WRITING & TEXT — CLIENT-SIDE

Two blocks of text, and exactly what changed between them. Lines are matched with a longest-common-subsequence table rather than a straight index-for-index comparison, so a line inserted at the top does not report every line below it as rewritten, and the counts satisfy the identity added + removed + unchanged = lines in A + lines in B − the length of the common subsequence. You get the added, removed and unchanged counts, the marked-up hunks with surrounding context, and a character-level similarity percentage from the same idea applied to individual characters.

LONGEST COMMON SUBSEQUENCE LINE DIFF CHARACTER SIMILARITY

Lines are matched with a real LCS table, not by asking whether line n of A equals line n of B. That is why inserting one line at the top does not mark every line below it as changed, and why added plus removed plus unchanged always adds back to the LCS identity.
