Regular Tetrahedron Calculator

MATH & GEOMETRY — CLIENT-SIDE

One edge length in, and every measure of the regular tetrahedron out. Each of the four faces is an equilateral triangle of area √3 a²/4, so the surface is √3 a² and the volume is a³/(6√2) — the two radii that matter are the inradius √6 a/12 and the circumradius √6 a/4, three times larger, with the midradius to an edge midpoint exactly twice the inradius. The dihedral angle is arccos(1/3), about 70.53°, and because it is a pure angle it must come out the same at every scale. The four vertices are given as coordinates with the centroid at the origin, at alternating corners of a cube of side √2 a/2, which you can check by measuring the distance between any two of them: all six must equal the edge you typed in.

The two enantiomorphic labelings of the cube's alternating corners both have every edge equal to a; the second is the first mirrored in z, so the two are congruent and either is a correct answer. What must hold either way: all four vertices sit at distance circumradius from the centre, every pairwise distance equals a, and the dihedral angle is the scale-free arccos(1/3) = 70.528779° at any edge length.
