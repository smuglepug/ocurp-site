Boolean Simplifier

MATH — CLIENT-SIDE

Enter a boolean expression and get its smallest equivalent form, proved by truth table rather than asserted. The parser is a hand-written tokeniser and shunting-yard evaluator — your text is never passed to eval() — and the simplification merges the on-set into the largest cubes, then re-checks the result against every one of the 2^n assignments before claiming equivalence.

Operators: AND OR NOT XOR NAND NOR, plus ( for grouping.

Result
Truth table — every assignment, in binary counting order

Simplification is by exact truth-table equivalence, not by pattern guessing: the page enumerates all 2n assignments and then finds the smallest set of terms that still matches. A rule like A AND (B OR NOT B) collapses to A because the two terms are absorption, and the page proves that by printing all four rows rather than asserting it.
