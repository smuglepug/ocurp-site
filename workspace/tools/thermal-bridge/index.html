Thermal Bridge

science — CLIENT-SIDE

A wall build-up, layer by layer. Each layer contributes R = d/lambda, the surface films add R_si and R_se, and U is one over the total. The linear transmittance PSI = f R_si U says how much extra heat the bridging element carries, and the page checks whether the internal surface drops below the dew point of the room - which is the part that actually damages the wall.

Surface temperature and condensation
A surface sweats when its temperature drops below the dew point of the room air. Dew point here comes from the Magnus formula g = ln(RH/100) + a T/(b + T), Td = b g/(a - g), with a = 17.62 and b = 243.12, valid over water between about -45 and 60 C. The surface drop is f R_si U (Ti - Te), and f is what decides everything: with the usual 0.03 for scattered fasteners a well-built wall loses a couple of hundredths of a kelvin and cannot possibly reach dew point. Push f towards 1, as it goes for an uninsulated steel or stainless element crossing the envelope, and the same wall goes cold enough to drip. That is what a thermal bridge is, and why the flag is worth having.
