Logistic Regression Solver

Ordinary logistic regression by Newton-Raphson on pasted x,y pairs: coefficients, log-likelihood, Wald z and p-values, and an honest report when the data are separated.

Ordinary logistic regression on pasted x,y pairs, fitted by Newton-Raphson — iteratively reweighted least squares — so the coefficients are the maximum-likelihood solution rather than a gradient-descent approximation of it. You get the slope and intercept, the log-likelihood, the 2×2 information matrix and its determinant, the standard errors from its inverse, the Wald z-statistics and two-sided p-values, and the confusion matrix with accuracy at the 0.5 cut. The honest part matters: if your data are perfectly separable — a line can split the two classes with no mistakes — then no finite maximum-likelihood estimate exists. The slope runs away instead of settling, the standard errors become numerical noise from a near-singular matrix, and this page says so in those words rather than printing a large number with six decimal places as though it meant something. Unparseable rows are skipped and counted, not coerced.

Standard errors come from the inverse observed information H-1, not from a linear regression formula: se(slope) = √(h00/det H) and se(intercept) = √(h11/det H). The p-values are two-sided Wald tests from the normal distribution. With fewer than about ten points the Wald test is not trustworthy at all — read the numbers as descriptive.
