Cantilever Deflection

science — CLIENT-SIDE

Euler-Bernoulli tip deflection for a rectangular cantilever, with the tip bending stress and a factor of safety against a yield strength you type in. Steel at 200 GPa or aluminium at 70 GPa, I = b h^3/12, and a flag the moment the sag passes L/360 - which is usually the limit that bites before the stress does.

Read-out

  

    What this assumes. Euler-Bernoulli beam
    theory: small deflections, linear elastic material, a prismatic rectangular
    section, and a point load at the tip of a cantilever fixed at the other
    end. Second moment of area I = b h^3 / 12 about the bending axis, tip
    deflection PL^3/(3EI), and tip bending stress 6PL/(b h^2). The L/360 limit
    is a serviceability rule of thumb for a member whose sag a person notices;
    it is a comfort criterion, not a strength criterion, and it is the one that
    usually bites first. Deflection scales with the cube of the length, so
    making a cantilever longer is enormously more expensive than making it
    stiffer, and orientation matters just as much: turning the section so the
    depth h sits along the bending axis multiplies I by (h/b)^2.
